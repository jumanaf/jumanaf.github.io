I believe I'm a great storyteller — it's something that is deeply ingrained in me, as someone who loves to read, write, and has taught lots of dense subjects over the years to wide audiences. Speaking at panels lately has me tapping back into this passion of mine: talking to an audience and learning through questions and discussion.

Separately, I have been ruminating over being a more precise person, generally, both as a culture for Kiwi and for my own personal growth. A lot of what I aspire to do comes from what I don't like to see: and lately, a huge pet peeve of mine has been imprecise language. Some people mask this by using words like "significant" or "trivial" because it _sounds_ like they are more precise, but I realize I look up to folks that use numbers and simple language to describe something. When you can describe something (ELI5) to someone in clear words, it means you aren't bullshitting — that creates trust within yourself, and with the people that you interface with.

As I've been writing more and describing our goals at Kiwi Charge, from levels of autonomy to how we shape customer experiences, I find it refreshing to write in explicit, rigorous language.

I believe blending this skill with my innate storytelling skill can be impactful for how I strategize and implement real engineering tasks. It has already helped me: when we talk about autonomous EV chargers, it felt incredibly overwhelming — but having precise language about what we want to accomplish means I can chip away at it — slowly but surely — and create something that people can trust in the real world.
